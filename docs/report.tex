\subhead{1. Architecture and protocol}
The service pools contributors' ETH and lets them vote on loans. A
\textbf{contributor} deposits, votes, withdraws disposable capital and claims
interest or compensation. An \textbf{applicant} proposes terms, resolves the
proposal and, if funded, receives and repays ETH. One account may hold both roles.

\textbf{On-chain design.} \entity{FundingPoolProxy} holds ETH and state;
\entity{FundingPool} supplies UUPS logic. Only the owner may upgrade it; tests
check state preservation. Each approved loan deploys a separate
\entity{LoanContract} with its own borrower, terms and repayment state.
Existing loan bytecode does not change when the pool is upgraded.
\entity{BitcoinOracle} stores satoshi balances and emits paid update requests.
The pool reads its stored balance during proposal resolution.

\textbf{Off-chain design.} The trusted Python oracle processes the first
\textbf{131,000 Bitcoin mainnet blocks}, one block at a time, rebuilding the
UTXO index \textbf{in memory}. It then answers Ethereum update requests.
Restarting repeats the scan: no Bitcoin balance/UTXO cache is stored on disk.
The separate voter detects proposal events and always votes YES, even with
fully locked capital. SQLite persists service events and signed transactions,
not Bitcoin balances; retries survive temporary RPC failures.

\textbf{Trust model.} BTC ownership is not proved and BTC are not locked.
Historical Bitcoin liquidity is evaluated at the fixed rate \textbf{1 BTC = 30 ETH}.
The parser checks connected blocks, hashes and Merkle roots, excludes the
unspendable genesis reward and refuses incomplete input; it is not a full
Bitcoin consensus node.

\subhead{Voting and funding}
Deposits must exceed \textbf{100,000 wei}. Voting eligibility includes locked
capital, but resolution weights each contributor's \textbf{current disposable
balance}; nonvoters count as NO and ties reject. Only the original applicant
may resolve, after at least \textbf{12 blocks}. Voting remains open until
resolution; \entity{votingDeadlineBlock} names the earliest resolution block.
All durations are block-height differences. Applicants specify principal,
interest rate (1--100), duration and a BTC address.

Insufficient disposable ETH or Bitcoin liquidity rejects and closes the
proposal. Approval closes voting and locks capital proportionally across
contributors. Integer-division remainders reduce the \textbf{actual disbursed
principal}; no arbitrary participant receives the difference. The loan fixes
its collateral percentage at creation.

\subhead{Repayment, interest and compensation}
Capital is either \textbf{disposable} or \textbf{locked}; claimable interest and
the compensation reserve are separate, non-loanable balances. Only the original
borrower may repay, including after default. Each installment covers principal
and interest in proportion to the total debt; one payment may repay everything.

\textbf{Principal refunds} unlock contributor capital in the pool, not directly
in their wallets. Refunds follow descending \emph{initial} loan shares, with
ties broken by descending numerical address. The order is sorted once.
\textbf{Interest} is split using the loan's fixed collateral percentage:
collateral enters the reserve; the rest becomes contributor gains proportional
to initial shares, collected through \entity{claimInterestGain()}.
Cumulative payment calculations preserve principal, interest and collateral
rounding across installments. Surplus above the full debt and interest
distribution dust enter the reserve. Page 4 gives a numerical example.

\textbf{Success/default.} Full repayment by the due block succeeds; an overdue,
unpaid loan is marked failed by a transaction, not by time passing alone.
Failure is counted once and cannot be reversed by late repayment. Global
collateral starts at \textbf{50\%}, stays within \textbf{1--100\%}, decreases
five points per success and increases five per failure; existing loans keep
their original percentages.

\textbf{Compensation} covers unpaid principal only, capped by the reserve.
An empty-reserve request still records default, pays zero and preserves the
claim for future refills. Actual compensation forfeits the same amount of
future principal recovery without changing original interest weights.
Only the owner may \textbf{terminate} the service, with no active loans;
withdrawal, interest claims and recovery remain available.

\pagetitle{2. Security and operational limits}

\subhead{Reentrancy: modification and executable attack}
The test-only \entity{FundingPoolVulnerable} inherits the real pool and replaces
withdrawal with stale balance writes \textbf{after} the external transfer:
\begin{codeblock}
uint256 beforePosition = totalContributed[msg.sender];
uint256 beforePool = totalUnlockedFunds;
(bool sent, ) = payable(msg.sender).call{value: amount}("");
if (!sent) revert TransferFailed();
totalContributed[msg.sender] = beforePosition - amount;
totalUnlockedFunds = beforePool - amount;
\end{codeblock}
\entity{ReentrancyAttacker} recursively calls \entity{withdraw(amount)} from
its receive function. In \entity{test/reentrancy.test.ts}, a 1 ETH attacker
deposit drains the honest contributor's 10 ETH from the vulnerable proxy;
the same attacker cannot steal those 10 ETH from the normal proxy, whose
accounting changes before transfer. Simply subtracting from a live balance
after transfer could revert on underflow in Solidity 0.8; stale assignments
make the modified example actually exploitable. Test variants are never deployed
by service setup. These checks are not a general security proof.

\subhead{Unfair remuneration without reentrancy}
A majority contributor with \textbf{6 ETH} can approve a \textbf{10 ETH} loan
to a colluding borrower, backed also by an honest contributor's \textbf{4 ETH},
then default. The colluding group captures a net \textbf{4 ETH}, not 10 ETH,
before gas and other flows. Any sufficiently funded BTC address can be cited
because ownership is not proved. Priority refunds also favor large positions:
with backing 6/4, a base repayment of 6 restores the larger position before
the smaller contributor recovers principal. These follow the protocol's
economic rules, rather than a reentrancy flaw.

\textbf{Limits.} Empty positions are removed, but work scales with contributors
and must fit block gas limits. Checkpoints detect chain replacement; nonce
conflicts remain inspectable. Reorganization recovery is not automatic.

\pagetitle{3. Run the service}
Run commands from the \textbf{project root}, unless indicated otherwise.
Use a supported even-numbered Node release (22.13.0+), Python 3.10+ and
\textbf{Geth 1.13.14}, the pre-PoS Clique client matching the private chain's genesis.

\subhead{1. Install dependencies and validate Bitcoin data}
\begin{codeblock}
./setup.sh
./.venv/bin/python python/oracle/check_data.py --data-dir bitcoin --summary
\end{codeblock}
Setup installs locked Node dependencies and Python packages, creates
\entity{.venv}, compiles contracts and obtains Geth. Installation and uncached
compiler downloads need internet access; skip setup if already completed.
Supply decoded mainnet \entity{blk*.dat} files containing genesis through
height 130,999. The offline checker needs no keys or Geth: success reports
\entity{valid: true}, \textbf{131,000 blocks} and height \textbf{130,999}, and
lists funded addresses. Missing/incomplete data must not be replaced by fake balances.

\subhead{2. Start the private chain / terminal 1}
\begin{codeblock}
cd private-chain
./geth --datadir=./data init project2526genesis.json
./geth --datadir=./data --keystore=./keystore --networkid=202526 \
  --http --http.addr=127.0.0.1 --http.port=8545 --http.api=eth,net,web3 \
  --mine --miner.gasprice=100000000 \
  --miner.etherbase=d278d247a52c550508ea2b2c9321d816238fb523 \
  --unlock d278d247a52c550508ea2b2c9321d816238fb523 \
  --password password.txt --allow-insecure-unlock --ipcdisable
\end{codeblock}
Keep the node running. The exposed RPC is local; insecure account unlocking
is for this private laboratory chain, not a public deployment.

\subhead{3. Create accounts and deploy / terminal 2, project root}
\begin{codeblock}
./.venv/bin/python python/setup.py
\end{codeblock}
The provided prefunded account \textbf{only transfers ETH} to three newly
generated accounts. A new deployer creates the oracle, implementation and
initialized proxy. Setup rebuilds ordinary bytecode, checks chain ID, receipts,
code and initialization, then writes protected service environments including
\entity{DEPLOYMENT\_BLOCK}. Repeating setup creates a separate deployment.

\subhead{4. Start the oracle and voter / separate terminals}
\begin{codeblock}
./.venv/bin/python python/oracle/oracle_service.py
./.venv/bin/python python/voter/auto_voter.py
\end{codeblock}
Wait for \textbf{ORACLE READY}: the oracle must finish its initial in-memory
Bitcoin scan. There is no fixed sleep to configure; restarting repeats the scan.
Keep the services running during the demo. Preserve each service's SQLite event
queue after temporary RPC failures; it is separate from Bitcoin indexing.

\subhead{5. Run repayment and default / another terminal, project root}
\begin{codeblock}
./.venv/bin/python python/simulate.py \
  --btc-address 1A1zP1eP5QGefi2DMPTfTL5SLmv7DivfNa --principal 1
\end{codeblock}
This concrete address has \textbf{4,000,000 satoshi} in the validated snapshot,
enough for 1 ETH at the fixed rate. With different data, choose a sufficiently
funded address from your checker summary; do not pass a literal placeholder.
Scenario A repays a 1 ETH loan at 5\% in two installments and claims gain.
Scenario B leaves 0.5 ETH unpaid, requests compensation and retries with an
empty reserve. Both proposals wait for YES votes and 12 real blocks.
Balances, loan states and accounting checks are printed after significant changes.

\textbf{Timing and recovery.} Clique blocks take about 10 seconds: allow several
minutes for both voting periods. \entity{--timeout} sets the maximum seconds
\emph{per asynchronous step} (default 300). On timeout, check node and service
logs before retrying; do not delete chain data, keys, environments or event queues.
The expected accounting is explained on page 4; reused deployments may differ.

\pagetitle{4. Demonstration: repayment and default}
This observed local run uses \entity{--principal 1}. The fresh oracle response
is \textbf{4,000,000 satoshi = 0.04 BTC}, equivalent to \textbf{1.2 ETH} of
capacity at 30 ETH/BTC. BTC are not transferred or locked. The contributor
deposits \textbf{50 ETH}; its wallet falls from 100 ETH to roughly 50 ETH
after gas, while the pool holds 50 ETH of disposable capital.

\subhead{First loan: 1 ETH, 5\% interest, two installments}
After the YES vote and 12-block period, approval creates and funds a dedicated
loan. The pool holds 49 ETH; the contributor has \textbf{49 disposable + 1 locked}.
The debt is \textbf{1.05 ETH}. With the loan's \textbf{50\% collateral}, each
0.525 ETH installment restores 0.5 ETH of principal and pays 0.025 ETH of
interest, divided equally between reserve and contributor gain:

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}Xrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Destination} & \textbf{Each installment (ETH)} & \textbf{Both (ETH)}\\\midrule
Principal restored to disposable capital & 0.5000 & 1.0000\\
Interest credited to compensation reserve & 0.0125 & 0.0250\\
Interest credited as contributor gain & 0.0125 & 0.0250\\\midrule
Borrower payment & 0.5250 & 1.0500\\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

The loan becomes \entity{Repaid}: disposable capital returns to 50 ETH and
locked capital to zero. The contributor claims the 0.025 ETH gain; the pool
retains \textbf{50 ETH capital + 0.025 ETH reserve}. Global collateral decreases
from 50\% to \textbf{45\%}, while the first loan's own percentage remains 50\%.

\subhead{Second loan: expiry does not execute a transaction}
A second proposal receives YES and is resolved after another 12-block period.
It disburses \textbf{0.5 ETH} at 10\% interest, with collateral fixed at
\textbf{45\%}, and is deliberately not repaid. It is funded at block
\textbf{328} and due at \textbf{331}. At block 332 it is overdue but still
stored as \entity{Funded}: merely passing a block height does not execute code.
The compensation transaction at block \textbf{333} records \entity{Defaulted},
reduces active loans to zero, counts one failure and raises global collateral
back to \textbf{50\%}. The second loan's own percentage stays 45\%.

\subhead{Compensation: limited reserve and preserved claim}
Unpaid principal is 0.5 ETH, but the reserve holds only 0.025 ETH. The contributor
receives \textbf{0.025 ETH directly in its wallet}; the remaining principal
claim is \textbf{0.475 ETH}. The retry at block \textbf{334} pays zero without
erasing this claim or counting the default twice.

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}Xrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Pool accounting (ETH)} & \textbf{Before compensation} & \textbf{After / retry}\\\midrule
Contributor total position & 50.000 & 49.975\\
Contributor disposable capital & 49.500 & 49.500\\
Contributor locked principal & 0.500 & 0.475\\
Compensation reserve & 0.025 & 0\\
Actual ETH held by the pool & 49.525 & 49.500\\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\textbf{Interpretation.} The 0.475 ETH locked position is an \textbf{unrecovered
claim, not withdrawable cash}. Compensation reduces both total position and
locked principal, leaving disposable capital unchanged:
$49.975-0.475=49.5$ ETH. Future reserve refills or late repayments may allow
further recovery, but it is not guaranteed. \textbf{Zero active loans does not
mean zero loss.}

\subhead{Wallet balances and transaction count}
The contributor's final wallet is \textbf{50.0499454037 ETH}, including gas:
interest gain and compensation arrived there, while 49.5 ETH remain disposable
in the pool. This run adds \textbf{11 demo transactions + 1 oracle update + 2 YES
votes = 14}; setup adds six (three transfers and three deployments).
The oracle holds 0.0000098852 ETH because an earlier unsuccessful address
request also paid its fee. That earlier request/response pair is outside this
run's 14 transactions. Block numbers and wallet values belong to this observed
run; reused deployments and different input can change them.

\subhead{5.1 Test execution and results}
After dependency setup (page 3), run from the \textbf{project root}.
\textbf{No Geth, oracle, voter, keys, service environments or real Bitcoin
files are needed.} Hardhat uses isolated simulated chains; Python tests use
temporary data and mocked external services. They do not alter the existing
private-chain deployment. Do not run \entity{python/setup.py} just to test.

\subhead{Compilation and complete test suites}
\begin{codeblock}
npm run typecheck
npm test
./.venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -s python/tests -v
\end{codeblock}
\textbf{Typecheck:} builds contracts and runs \entity{tsc --noEmit}; successful
exit with no errors is sufficient, even if TypeScript prints nothing.
\textbf{Contracts:} expect \textbf{34 passing}, comprising \textbf{4 Solidity
+ 30 Node.js/TypeScript tests}. A Solidity fuzz test uses \textbf{256 generated
inputs}; these are repetitions of one test, not 256 extra tests.
\textbf{Python:} expect \textbf{Ran 49 tests} and \textbf{OK}.

Contract scenarios cover voting boundaries and changing balances, BTC thresholds,
allocation dust, installments, refund order, interest, defaults, repeated
compensation, access, termination, upgrades and reentrancy. Explicit checks
validate event emitters/arguments and independent contracts for two borrowers.
Python checks cover parsing, genesis exclusion, UTXOs, atomic indexing, durable
event/transaction retries, the always-YES voter, setup safeguards and demo
compensation. Mocked balances test contract logic, not the real Bitcoin dataset.

\subhead{Select a focused check}
\begin{codeblock}
npm test -- test/advanced_behaviors.test.ts
npm test -- test/events_and_isolation.test.ts
npm test -- test/reentrancy.test.ts
npm test -- test/upgrade.test.ts
npm test -- contracts/test/LoanContract.t.sol
./.venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -s python/tests \
  -p 'test_demo_scenarios.py' -v
\end{codeblock}
Choose the relevant command: accounting; events and loan isolation; reentrancy;
upgrades; native loan rules; or \textbf{six offline demo checks}. Event checks
include a compensation retry that must not emit another default. Isolation
checks repay two loans with different borrowers and ensure that neither changes
the other loan's terms, repayments or contributor positions.

\subhead{Inspect code coverage}
\begin{codeblock}
npm run test:coverage
\end{codeblock}
\textbf{Coverage:} expect \textbf{34 passing} and line/statement percentages.
Open \entity{coverage/html/index.html} in a browser. This is contract coverage,
not Python coverage or proof of security. Instrumented bytecode changes gas:
coverage deliberately leaves ordinary gas measurements unchanged. The profiling
commands and measurements are on page 6; do not use coverage gas for costs.

\subhead{Recognize success and troubleshoot}
A test may deliberately trigger a revert and still pass: it checks rejection
of an invalid operation. The reentrancy test passes only if the unsafe variant
is drained \emph{and} the normal pool protects honest funds. Read the final
summary; failed assertions, \entity{FAIL}/\entity{ERROR} or a nonzero exit status
need investigation. Counts describe this version and may change as tests are added.

\textbf{Missing modules/interpreter:} check the working directory, supported Node
version and completed \entity{./setup.sh}; use \entity{./.venv/bin/python}.
\textbf{Failed test:} rerun its file and inspect the first expected/actual mismatch.
Do not delete private-chain data or event queues to fix isolated tests.
\textbf{Missing Bitcoin input:} repair the dataset for the separate checker/demo
(page 3); passing mocked tests does not validate those files. The real demo is
an integration exercise, not a substitute for automated tests or vice versa.

\subhead{Read the values and calibrate the oracle fee}
Values are \textbf{gas units}, not wei and not the submitted transaction gas
limit. The fee in wei is \textbf{gas used times gas price}. Getters invoked
through \entity{eth\_call} incur no paid transaction fee.

\textbf{Oracle fee:} $49,426\times0.1\text{ gwei}=4,942,600,000,000$ wei.
Calibration uses a first nonzero update of a 34-character historical address;
existing-slot updates cost less. Bytecode/settings changes require recalibration.

\textbf{Scope.} These are reproducible scenario measurements, not universal
upper bounds. Costs depend on storage state and contributor count; in
particular, pool work can grow with the number of contributors. Coverage uses
instrumented bytecode, so it exercises these paths without overwriting the
ordinary gas JSON. Use the profiling command above for cost evaluation.
