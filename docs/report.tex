\pagetitle{02 / PROTOCOL}{Lifecycle \& accounting}{Rules applied by the pool and each dedicated loan contract.}

\subhead{1. Proposal, voting and resolution}
\textbf{Contributor.} Deposits must exceed \textbf{100,000 wei}. A contributor
retains voting eligibility even when all capital is locked. Each vote is YES or NO;
abstentions count as NO. At resolution, the pool recomputes weights using each
contributor's \emph{current disposable balance}; ties reject.

\textbf{Applicant.} Specifies principal, interest rate (1--100), duration in blocks
and a Bitcoin address. Only the original applicant resolves the proposal, after
at least \textbf{12 blocks}. Voting stays open until resolution.
The ABI field \entity{votingDeadlineBlock} denotes the earliest resolution block.

\textbf{FundingPool.} Insufficient disposable ETH or BTC liquidity rejects and
closes the proposal. Approval also closes voting and creates a new
\entity{LoanContract}. All time periods use block-height differences.

\subhead{2. Proportional allocation and loan terms}
The pool locks each contributor's proportional share of current disposable funds.
Integer-division remainders reduce the \textbf{actual disbursed principal}; they
are not assigned to an arbitrary participant. The loan snapshots the current
collateral percentage, which remains fixed for that loan.

\subhead{3. Installments, principal refunds and interest}
Only the original borrower may repay an active or failed loan. Each installment
contains both principal and interest. With principal $P$, rate $r$, total interest
$I=\lfloor Pr/100\rfloor$ and cumulative accepted payment $C\leq P+I$:
\begin{notebox}
\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{3pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{5pt}
\[
\underbrace{B(C)=\left\lfloor\frac{CP}{P+I}\right\rfloor}_{\text{cumulative principal}},
\qquad
\underbrace{J(C)=C-B(C)}_{\text{cumulative interest}}.
\]
\small Each installment applies the change in these targets. Collateral uses
changes in $\lfloor Jc/100\rfloor$, so splitting a payment preserves the
cumulative principal/interest and collateral rounding totals.
\end{notebox}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Principal:} refunds positions from highest to lowest \emph{initial}
loan share, breaking ties by descending numerical address. Merge sort computes
the order once; pre-upgrade loans without an order are prepared lazily.
\item \textbf{Interest:} collateral enters the compensation pool; gains are
credited separately in proportion to original loan shares and withdrawn using
\entity{claimInterestGain()}. Distribution dust also enters compensation.
\item \textbf{Overpayment:} any amount beyond the entire principal-plus-interest
debt enters compensation. A single installment may repay the whole debt.
\end{itemize}

\subhead{4. Success, default and compensation}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.488\linewidth}
\begin{notebox}\small
{\bfseries\color{Teal}FUNDED $\rightarrow$ REPAID}\par
Full repayment by the due block is successful. Global collateral decreases by
five points. The loan closes; recovered capital becomes disposable again.
\end{notebox}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.488\linewidth}
\begin{tcolorbox}[panel,colback=Sand,borderline west={1.5pt}{0pt}{Warm}]\small
{\bfseries\color{Warm}OVERDUE $\rightarrow$ DEFAULTED}\par
Late repayment or a valid default/compensation request records failure once.
Global collateral increases by five points. Later repayment cannot reverse it.
\end{tcolorbox}
\end{minipage}

Collateral starts at \textbf{50\%} and is bounded to \textbf{1--100\%}. Compensation
covers unpaid principal only, up to the available reserve. A valid request with
an empty reserve records default but pays zero; the claim remains for later
refills. Compensation forfeits the same amount of future principal recovery,
while original interest weights remain unchanged.

\begin{notebox}\small
\textbf{Lifecycle control.} Only the owner may upgrade the pool or terminate it
when no active loans remain. Termination preserves recovery operations. Existing
loan contracts keep their original bytecode after an upgrade. The installment
split, minimum voting period and empty-reserve behavior are documented
interpretations of the trace's less explicit wording.
\end{notebox}

\pagetitle{04 / SECURITY}{Attacks \& operational limits}{Required adversarial demonstration and explicit trust assumptions.}

\subhead{Reentrancy: intentionally changing the real pool}
The test-only \entity{FundingPoolVulnerable} inherits \entity{FundingPool} and
changes withdrawal: it caches the position, transfers ETH first, then writes
stale balances. \entity{ReentrancyAttacker} recursively calls
\entity{withdraw(amount)} from its receive function.

\begin{codeblock}
uint256 beforePosition = totalContributed[msg.sender];
uint256 beforePool = totalUnlockedFunds;
(bool sent, ) = payable(msg.sender).call{value: amount}("");
if (!sent) revert TransferFailed();
totalContributed[msg.sender] = beforePosition - amount;
totalUnlockedFunds = beforePool - amount;
\end{codeblock}

\begin{minipage}[t]{0.488\linewidth}
\begin{tcolorbox}[panel,colback=Sand,borderline west={1.5pt}{0pt}{Warm}]\small
{\bfseries\color{Warm}VULNERABLE VARIANT}\par
The same claim remains available during reentry. A 1 ETH attacker deposit can
drain the other contributor's 10 ETH. The test observes an empty victim pool.
\end{tcolorbox}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.488\linewidth}
\begin{notebox}\small
{\bfseries\color{Teal}NORMAL FUNDING POOL}\par
Accounting changes before the external call. Reentry fails while the legitimate
withdrawal finishes. The honest contributor's 10 ETH remain in the pool.
\end{notebox}
\end{minipage}

The same attacker is tested against both proxies. Simply moving a checked live
subtraction after the transfer might underflow and revert in Solidity 0.8;
stale assignments make this modified example actually exploitable. Test variants
live in \entity{contracts/test/} and are never deployed by the service setup.
These checks do not constitute a general security proof.

\subhead{Unfair remuneration without reentrancy}
\begin{notebox}
\textbf{Majority contributor: 6 ETH}\quad +\quad
\textbf{honest minority: 4 ETH}\quad $\rightarrow$\quad \textbf{10 ETH loan}
\end{notebox}
The majority contributor can approve a loan to a colluding borrower and default.
The attacker moves 6 ETH of its own capital but captures the minority's 4 ETH:
the net gain is \textbf{4 ETH}, not the full loan. Any sufficiently funded BTC
address can be cited because the trace requires no ownership proof. Priority
refunds also favor large initial positions: with backing 6/4, a base repayment
of 6 restores the whale's position before the smaller contributor recovers any
principal. These are consequences of the specified economic rules.

\subhead{Boundaries of the implementation}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bitcoin oracle.} Applies blocks in chain order and checks payload
hashes/Merkle roots, but trusts historical input rather than implementing full
Bitcoin consensus. It excludes the unspendable genesis reward and publishes
nothing after failed/incomplete synchronization.
\item \textbf{Service reliability.} SQLite persists requests and signed
transactions before advancing/broadcasting. Services retry temporary failures;
checkpoints detect chain replacement and nonce conflicts remain inspectable.
The target is a single-node private chain, without automatic reorganization recovery.
\item \textbf{Scale and upgrades.} Empty contributor positions are removed and
refund order is sorted once. Work still grows with contributor count and must
fit the block gas limit. V2 tests preserve ownership, state and active positions;
previously created loan contracts remain immutable.
\end{itemize}

\subhead{1. Install and validate Bitcoin input}
Use a supported even-numbered Node release (22.13.0+), Python 3.10+ and Geth
\textbf{1.13.14} for the supplied Clique genesis. Run from the project root:
\begin{codeblock}
./setup.sh
./.venv/bin/python python/oracle/check_data.py --data-dir bitcoin --summary
\end{codeblock}
\entity{setup.sh} installs dependencies and Geth, creates the Python environment
and compiles; deployment is separate. Supply decoded mainnet \entity{blk*.dat}.
The checker must report \entity{valid: true}, 131,000 blocks and height 130,999;
it lists funded addresses without needing keys or an Ethereum connection.

\begin{codeblock}
cd private-chain
./geth --datadir=./data init project2526genesis.json
./geth --datadir=./data --keystore=./keystore --networkid=202526 \
  --http --http.addr=127.0.0.1 --http.port=8545 --http.api=eth,net,web3 \
  --mine --miner.gasprice=100000000 --miner.etherbase=d278d247a52c550508ea2b2c9321d816238fb523 \
  --unlock d278d247a52c550508ea2b2c9321d816238fb523 \
  --password password.txt --allow-insecure-unlock --ipcdisable
\end{codeblock}

\subhead{3. Deploy the service / terminal 2, project root}
\begin{codeblock}
./.venv/bin/python python/setup.py
\end{codeblock}
The prefunded account \textbf{only transfers ETH} to new accounts. A new deployer
creates the oracle, implementation and initialized proxy. Setup rebuilds bytecode,
validates the chain and deployment, then writes service environments including
\entity{DEPLOYMENT\_BLOCK}. Each run creates a separate deployment.

\subhead{4. Start both Python services / separate terminals}
\begin{codeblock}
./.venv/bin/python python/oracle/oracle_service.py
./.venv/bin/python python/voter/auto_voter.py
\end{codeblock}
Wait for \entity{ORACLE READY}. At each start, the oracle reconstructs its UTXO
index \textbf{in memory}, one block at a time, over the required first 131,000
blocks. No Bitcoin cache is stored on disk. Keep each service's SQLite event
database when restarting after an RPC failure.

\subhead{5. Run both loan scenarios / project root}
\begin{codeblock}
./.venv/bin/python python/simulate.py \
  --btc-address YOUR_VALIDATED_ADDRESS --principal 1
\end{codeblock}
Choose an address from the validated summary. The demo checks a fresh oracle
balance and contributes. Scenario A funds 1 ETH at 5\%, repays two 0.525 ETH
installments and claims gain. Scenario B funds 0.5 ETH, waits beyond its three-block
term and requests compensation, recording default. On a fresh deployment the
reserve pays 0.025 ETH; a zero-reserve retry preserves the remaining 0.475 ETH
claim. Receipts and compensation invariants are checked; balances, deadlines and
loan states are printed. Both proposals wait for real YES votes and 12 blocks.
